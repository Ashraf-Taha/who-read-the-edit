## 8. Limitations

**Scale.** The main experiment uses three models of 0.5B to 1.7B parameters from two families. {{scope.7b}} repeat it in bfloat16 on the same questions, because they do not fit in 32-bit floats on our machine. Long contexts, the synthetic agent traces, the needle task, the long new input and the runtime were run on Qwen2.5-1.5B only. Larger models may concentrate or spread the effect of an edit differently.

**No serving engine.** We measure closeness to a re-prefill, the share of tokens recomputed and relative cost in an unoptimised reference engine. We do not measure latency or throughput in vLLM or SGLang, and we have not written a fused kernel; the roofline estimate of Appendix A assumes kernels at peak and is no substitute for a measurement. Relocation by offset groups and per-segment exposure sums were measured only in unfused PyTorch, where call overheads dominate, and recomputing scattered tokens may cost a larger share of a re-prefill in a fused engine than in ours.

**Baselines.** Our CacheBlend criterion is LMCache's default single-layer rule with small changes, not the paper's layer-by-layer refinement; ProphetKV's score is run with heads averaged; and EPIC's selection is run as a fixed window at the start of each document. We did not run the region refresh of Yu et al. or a trained method such as KVEraser's adapter, and faithful reimplementations could do better than the rules we ran. The strongest open comparisons are with region refresh, which Yu et al. find far better than isolated tokens on exact match [@yu2026budgeted], and with contiguous windows extended to a document boundary, which Mao et al. favour when the dependent text is adjacent [@mao2026contiguity].

**Workloads and metric.** The edits are single documents in multi-document question answering, {{scope.trace_edits}}, and a needle task. In the main experiment the new input is only the few tokens that open the reply, and the cached question sometimes sits inside the user message with documents after it, an unusual prompt for an instruction-tuned model. Replies are short, so the main metric is dominated by the first reply tokens; KL without the first token and a long-reply variant (Appendix C) address this only in part. Our metric is KL to the re-prefill, while the closest prior work reports exact match and answer margins, so the numbers are not directly comparable. {{scope.transcripts_limit}}

**Erasure.** A repaired cache is close to the re-prefill for the next answer, not equal to it, and Section 5.5 shows that cached readers keep part of what they took from deleted content. Our probes of leakage are narrow (one answer, one quotation request and one needle task) and cannot show that nothing else leaks.

**Statistics.** The main experiment has 36 questions per model, each in three layouts, and its per-stratum tests have 12 or 24 questions. With 12 questions the smallest two-sided Wilcoxon $p$ is about $5 \times 10^{-4}$, and power for the short-suffix tests is low. Means of KL are heavy-tailed and a few cases carry much of each mean, so we also report medians, per-case distributions and ratios with intervals, and we Holm-correct within each family but not across families.
