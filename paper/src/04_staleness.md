## 4. Where staleness lives

### 4.1 Workloads

Each prompt holds the ten documents of a HotpotQA question (at least six for a few questions) and the question, in a chat template, within 2,200 tokens [@yang2018hotpotqa]. We delete one document $E$ that is neither first nor last and at least 16 tokens long: either one of the two *supporting* documents the answer needs, or a *distractor*. Read in reverse, the same cases describe a document that arrives late and is inserted. A second workload uses running text, a WikiText-103 article [@merity2017wikitext] with about 400 tokens before the edited paragraph and 600 after it, and the article's next 32 tokens as new input. For each case we compute three encodings of the suffix. The *cached* one was encoded with $E$ present, as the cache holds it; the *gapped* one was encoded without $E$ but at its old positions; and the *closed* one was encoded without $E$ and moved up against the prefix, which is the re-prefill. Appendix B gives group sizes and lengths.

### 4.2 Readers carry the damage

The cleanest test reads each case twice, with the same tokens and only the split between cache and new input changed. Either the question arrives after the edit, as new input computed over the relocated stale documents, or it was asked before the edit and is part of the stale suffix. Table {{#paired_reader}} gives the result for 80 supporting-document deletions per model ({{e1p.qwen1.5b.questions}} distinct questions).

{{table:paired_reader}}

In both readings the documents after the edit absorbed the deleted content; what differs is whether the answer is computed from a token that absorbed it. When the question is fresh, reuse stays close to the re-prefill even though the deletion itself moves the answer a lot: on Qwen2.5-1.5B the KL of reuse is {{e1p.qwen1.5b.fresh_of_effect}} of the deletion's own effect. When the same question was cached it is {{e1p.qwen1.5b.cached_of_effect}}, {{e1p.qwen1.5b.ratio}} times higher, and {{e1p.qwen1.5b.same.cached}} answers match the re-prefill's against {{e1p.qwen1.5b.same.fresh}} (McNemar $p$ = {{e1p.qwen1.5b.same.p}}). The other two models agree (Table {{#paired_reader}}). Li reaches the same conclusion by causal intervention: after a field changes, the decision reads conclusions written onto downstream tokens at prefill, and the field's own cells matter little [@li2026notes].

Exposure picks out the cells that moved. For each suffix token we compare its exposure with how far its cells moved because of what they read, measured between the cached and gapped encodings, which share positions so that geometry drops out. The rank correlation is {{e1.rho_mean}} on average over {{e1.cases}} cases and the three models, and at least {{e1.rho_p10}} in 90% of cases. The 10% of suffix tokens with the most exposure hold {{e1.share10.exposure_mean}} of the total cell deviation, against {{e1.share10.oracle}} for the best possible 10%, {{e1.share10.boundary_window}} for the 10% right after the edit and {{e1.share10.tail_window}} for the last 10%. So exposure tells which cells are stale. It does not tell which of them matter for the answer, and a repair needs both.

### 4.3 Leaving a gap is not free

A vLLM proposal for context editing keeps positions as they are after a deletion, because renumbering would invalidate the blocks downstream [@vllm58537], and Prompt Cache states that output quality is unaffected as long as relative positions are kept [@gim2024promptcache]. We tested this with no staleness at all: in an unedited context, with every cell exact, we shift the positions of everything from one document onward by a gap $g$ (Table {{#gap}}).

{{table:gap}}

A gap of one position does almost nothing (mean KL at most {{e15.one.max}}). Larger gaps hurt on all three models, by {{e15.64.min}} to {{e15.64.max}} nats at 64 positions and {{e15.1024.min}} to {{e15.1024.max}} at 1,024, where up to {{e15.max_changed}} of greedy answers change. The deleted documents in these workloads average {{e1.qwen0.5b.edit_min}} to {{e1.qwen0.5b.edit_max}} tokens, depending on the group, so deleting one leaves a gap of that size, and there the gap can matter more than staleness. In the cases of Section 4.1, recomputing every suffix cell exactly at the gapped positions leaves the output further from the re-prefill than reusing the stale cells at closed-up positions, significantly in {{e1.hole.worse}} of the {{e1.hole.groups}} groups with a fresh reader ({{e1.hole.worse_holm}} after Holm correction; Appendix B). If the reference is the edited text with ordinary positions, as we assume, not renumbering is an approximation separate from staleness. These models were never trained on gapped inputs, and group position encoding shows that such tolerance has to be trained in [@tong2025streaming].
